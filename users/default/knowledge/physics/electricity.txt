Electricity

Electricity is a name for effects caused by electric charge. A flow of charge through a wire is an electric current. This flow can transfer energy to a lamp and make it light up.

Voltage measures energy for each unit of electric charge. You can think of voltage as an electrical push that can make current flow around a complete circuit. Voltage is measured in volts.

Resistance is how much a material opposes an electric current. A larger resistance lets less current flow at the same voltage. Resistance is measured in ohms.

A circuit is a complete path for electric current. Opening a switch breaks the path and stops the current in a simple battery circuit. Use only suitable low-voltage learning kits with adult help; do not experiment with wall sockets.

For an ohmic resistor at constant temperature, Ohm's law is V = I R. Here V is voltage in volts, I is current in amperes, and R is resistance in ohms. Electrical power transferred to a component is P = V I, in watts, using the passive sign convention.
