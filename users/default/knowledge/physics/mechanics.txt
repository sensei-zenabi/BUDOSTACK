Newton's laws of motion

Newton's first law says an object remains at rest or keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed unless an overall external force changes its motion. A force is a push or a pull. The tendency to keep the same motion is called inertia.

Simple explanation: Newton's first law says things keep moving because an object does not need a push to keep a steady speed in a straight line. A push or pull changes that motion. A rolling ball slows down because friction pushes against its movement.

Newton's second law says that a larger overall force makes the same mass accelerate more. Acceleration means a change in velocity, including speed or direction. For a fixed mass, force equals mass times acceleration: F = m a.

Newton's third law says forces come in pairs acting on different objects. When you push a wall, the wall pushes back on you with an equal force in the opposite direction. The forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel on one object.
