Newton's laws of motion

Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or moves at constant velocity in a straight line unless acted on by a nonzero net external force. This is the law of inertia. A balanced set of forces produces no acceleration in an inertial reference frame.

Newton's second law relates net force to the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass, the relation is F = m a: net force equals mass times acceleration. Force is measured in newtons, mass in kilograms, and acceleration in metres per second squared.

Newton's third law states that when one body exerts a force on another, the second exerts an equal and opposite force on the first. These two forces act on different bodies, so they do not cancel in a force balance for one body.
